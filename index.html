Expert content review

You are rating candidate items for a new instrument, the Collective Political Agency scale (CPA). The scale measures an individually held perception of collective capacity: how the us is experienced from the individual's point of view. It is a state measure with a 1-month reference window.

Construct definitions

Your three tasks per item. (1) Relevance: how relevant is this item to the construct system as a whole? (2) Clarity: how clearly would you expect a community member to understand it? (3) Sorting: to which construct does the item read as belonging? Items are presented in shuffled order without their intended labels. A comment box is provided for suggested rewording or comments. Item-level content validity (I-CVI) will be computed as the proportion of experts rating relevance 3 or 4; items below .78 will be revised or dropped (Polit & Beck, 2006; Lynn, 1986). Your sort assignments are used diagnostically — to identify which construct an item is read as reflecting — rather than scored against published benchmarks, which are normed on large panels of raters drawn from the general respondent population (Colquitt et al., 2019). A separate definitional-correspondence study with raters drawn from the instrument's intended respondent population will follow (Hinkin & Tracey, 1999). A final section following the items assesses the content coverage of the item pool — a judgment for which expert raters are specifically recommended (Colquitt et al., 2019).

Assessment of content coverage

For each construct, rate the extent to which the 20 items, considered as a set, sample the full content domain of the definition (Cronbach, 1990; Nunnally, 1978; Colquitt et al., 2019). This judgment concerns the adequacy of the pool as a whole rather than the quality of individual items. Where sampling appears incomplete, please describe the unrepresented aspects of the domain and, where possible, suggest candidate items.
